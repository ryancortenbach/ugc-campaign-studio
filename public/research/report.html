AI UGC audience and business research

Build an original character for people who recognize gaming subcultures, social rituals, and small status games. Use William Maxxer to understand that audience, Luca Maxiim to study a repeatable character-led production format, and Children of Khan to study how a fictional world connects to apparel. These are three distinct references. Copying William’s face, Luca’s character, or Khan’s slogans would discard the strongest opportunity: a recognizable world that belongs to this project.

The product has three commercial surfaces: original T-shirts, a $97 AI production course, and a recurring brand-avatar service targeting at least ten content pieces daily. The useful common asset is a production system that can retain identity, review prompts before generation, turn mistakes into reusable checks, and measure actual commercial results.

Evidence and coverage

The collection contains 400 entries returned for the William profile and 400 for the Luca profile, collected October 24, 2026. It includes collaboration entries, so input-profile counts differ from owner-account counts. William’s sample spans December 14, 2025 through October 23, 2026. Luca’s covers September 25 through October 24, 2026. Each query was capped at 400, so this is a large defined sample, not a claim that every historical post was retrieved.

The 30 selected William entries account for about 56.3% of the collected sample’s lifetime plays. The sample has 23 entries above one million plays. These totals are a snapshot, not monthly reach.

Thirty William videos were selected by descending public play/view count and downloaded without re-encoding from the returned source variants. Twelve Luca examples were downloaded by the same rule. Three frames from each of those 42 videos were inspected, covering 126 reference frames. Machine transcription succeeded for 408 of those 412 selected sources. Four downloaded sources contain no audio stream and are explicitly marked for visual/text review. Music, silence, visual jokes, and unusual game terminology can produce transcription errors. Short or suspicious transcripts are flagged in the index rather than treated as reliable dialogue.

Public lifetime play counts are neither unique viewers nor monthly impressions. Older posts have had more time to accumulate views. A top-view ranking identifies attention winners, not conversion winners. No retention graph, audience demographic dashboard, paid-ad result, customer order ledger, or private conversion data was obtained.

The exact per-reel metrics, source links, timestamps, hashes, and coverage diagnostics accompany this report. The local reference library preserves the source media separately from proposed original work.

What William’s audience recognizes

Games as ordinary social life

A central recurring device treats game worlds as everyday life. A fantasy merchant becomes a dishonest shopkeeper, a dungeon trip becomes a questionable night out, and fictional currency becomes an excuse for financial-status behavior. The viewer gets the joke by recognizing a precise location, object, mechanic, or social role.

The important transferable skill is specificity. A generic joke about gamers is weak. An observation about someone treating a trivial server promotion like a real management position is instantly legible to the right audience. The relevant niche is people with a shared vocabulary and memory, not simply everyone who plays games.

Examples include the Falkreath blacksmith anecdote, fantasy relationship setup, and Draugr reference. These examples are evidence for the mechanism; a commercial world should use original locations, characters, and product stories.

Role-play communities and borrowed authority

Garry’s Mod and role-play-server culture supplies a particularly rich set of social contradictions: requesting permission to speak, ranks that feel more important than real responsibilities, elaborate rules, and interpersonal drama inside an invented institution. The comic tension is between how seriously the character treats the institution and how small the actual stakes are.

The military RP example and permission-to-speak example make this mechanism clear. A brand-safe adaptation can use an invented neighborhood club, a fictional guild office, or an overorganized group chat. It should preserve the social observation instead of reproducing a creator’s exact anecdote.

Digital status, economies, and reputation

TF2, Steam, Counter-Strike, inventories, cosmetics, and bans are treated as social status systems. The comedy comes from tiny differences that insiders understand: an inexpensive item carries outsized prestige, a digital reputation follows someone around, or a person critiques everybody else’s appearance despite an unimpressive setup.

The VAC-history example is a useful structural reference. The original product translation is a character who acts as if a small everyday possession is a legendary item. Apparel, desk accessories, keyboards, cases, and collectibles can all enter this world naturally.

Nostalgia and precise remembered behavior

Childhood habits, early internet communities, older streetwear, and forgotten social rituals are another source of recognition. The elementary-school behavior video is the largest public-play example in this sample. It relies on an unusually specific shared memory rather than an elaborate plot.

The 2016 streetwear reference suggests a useful visual lane: familiar silhouettes, slightly dated accessories, and the confidence of someone who never stopped dressing for a particular era. This can inform an original wardrobe without copying William’s real appearance.

Impressions, relationships, and everyday observations

The broader feed also includes dating impressions, restaurant interactions, cash-payment jokes, cars, music trends, gym behavior, mirror effects, and short visual setups. These widen the audience beyond gaming.

Several of the highest-play examples depend on profanity, ethnic caricature, sexual material, harassment-style encounters, or other context that is a poor default for a DTC brand. Raw popularity should not be the only selection rule. The strongest commercial transfer is the timing, confidence, recognizability, and sharp observation. A brand should choose the subject matter deliberately.

Other recurring lanes in the wider feed

The wider sample also includes Half-Life, Gears of War, Fortnite and Minecraft references; TV-character reenactments; music and lip-sync trends; AI-assistant roast sketches; appearance-ranking and looksmaxxing vocabulary; creator culture and reaction-GIF jokes; celebrity impressions; public-interaction sketches; and a recurring relationship persona using Filipina/Pinay cultural tropes. Those are descriptions of the content presented, not claims about the creator’s private relationships or a recommendation to copy those tropes.

This broader map matters when choosing scenes and clothing. Gaming references support the LAN room, fictional guild office and fantasy market. TV and nostalgic character behavior support a distinctive costume silhouette. AI-assistant sketches suggest a recurring off-screen foil. Consumer wordplay works in a cafe, car, shop or ordinary apartment. Music/trend clips need a visual concept and sound-rights decision rather than a forced spoken script.

Audience hypothesis

A practical starting hypothesis is an English-speaking, internet-fluent adult audience with overlapping interests in games, nostalgia, clothes, desk setups, and absurd social observation. An initial 18-30 creative target is a testing choice, not a verified demographic fact from Instagram analytics. Start with this hypothesis, then ask actual target viewers through Terac which concepts feel recognizably theirs.

The visual grammar to carry forward

William’s selected references are often simple: close phone framing, a car interior or familiar everyday setting, direct address, a short text hook near the top, and a niche visual insert that explains the reference. Clothes are casual and recognizable, and expression does much of the work. The production feels immediate rather than ceremonious.

Luca’s selected references instead repeatedly show a stylized game-world presenter, consistent costume and accessories, varied locations, secondary characters, cuts into close-ups, a recurring payoff, and an apparel or site plug. The character’s shirt remains part of the video world rather than appearing only as a conventional product card. Some selected entries are live action, so the account should not be described as exclusively AI-generated.

Combine William’s conversational intimacy with Luca’s consistent fictional world. Keep the first shot easy to read. Use a specific observation, one escalation, and one payoff. Let the product enter because it belongs in the character’s life.

An original character and world

The following are proposed creative directions, not recovered facts or approved final artwork. The previously accepted Higgsfield character remains preserved in the separate avatar dossier.

Character bible: Milo, working concept

Milo is an original fictional adult in his mid-twenties. He treats minor everyday decisions like important game quests, with quiet confidence and occasional self-awareness. He knows the lore of his own world, but he is not an infallible guru. The humor should sometimes land on him.

Use an original face reference with a clearly different face and hair silhouette from William and Luca’s presenter. A proposed design direction is warm brown skin, short dark textured hair, a compact athletic build, and a slightly raised outer eyebrow. These are design choices awaiting an image study. Do not substitute a description for an approved reference image.

His performance is restrained: eyes open and alert, a short glance at the prop, a return to the camera, and a small satisfied reaction. Keep head movement and hands simple. A calm original conversational voice with natural pauses fits the audience. The final accent and voice must be selected explicitly, rather than cloning either reference creator.

Lock the approved face, hair, skin, proportions, clothing construction, garment print, voice ID, and reference versions. Secondary characters should have their own approved references. Store the bible independently from scripts so a new script cannot accidentally redefine the character.

Keep pants and shoes simple so the product remains recognizable. Generate and approve front, side, back, and close product views before motion.

Shot and prompt architecture

A five-second prospect sample should prove a visual idea, not cram in a full sales pitch. Use one short hook, one clear product moment, and a readable final frame. Keep dialogue around ten to fifteen words unless an actual voice reference demonstrates that a different pace works.

A practical five-second structure is: 0-1 seconds, immediate recognizable setup; 1-4 seconds, one action or product reveal; 4-5 seconds, reaction and brand frame. Keep the object stationary if hand-object interaction does not add value. Do not introduce multiple characters, a location change, and a long line into the same five seconds.

For a longer post, use a short establishing shot, a conversational medium shot, one insert, and a payoff close-up. The exact timeline must come from the actual voice performance. Historical avatar work repeatedly failed on sleepy eyes, furniture drift, an inconsistent secondary character, ambiguous hand gestures, stale reference tags, and rushed final dialogue. Those are concrete review categories, not vague quality preferences.

The implemented review adapter can run up to three specialist rounds with at most two prompt revisions. Missing context blocks model spending. Any failed reviewer leaves the prompt blocked. Every reviewer must score at least 85 and leave no major or blocking issue for the deterministic final gate to pass. A pass means ready for human/spend review, not a guarantee that the render will succeed.

Bind the report to the exact prompt, reference manifest, and settings fingerprint. Changing any of them invalidates the old report. Keep a cost ledger and a maximum generation budget. Render review remains a separate step because text review cannot inspect a video that does not exist yet.

The three offers and their funnel

Original apparel

The reference model is narrative-led merchandise: repeated visual identity makes the garment recognizable, then the collection page converts that recognition into a purchase. Khan’s storefront provides a public example of tee pricing around $60, higher-priced layers, a shipping threshold, and a multi-item bundle. These are observed offer mechanics, not evidence of our conversion rate.

Start with a narrow collection and an identifiable print. Measure video-to-profile visits, site sessions, product views, add-to-cart, checkout completion, retained orders, returns, and contribution after fulfillment. A strong view count with little qualified site traffic is an attention result, not a clothing business result.

The $97 course

The Elite Ball Knowledge reference uses a dark, centered sales page, a large headline, a video-led demonstration, method steps, proof, a one-time offer, and FAQs. Its current advertised price is $197. Our mock uses Ryan’s $97 price and original copy, and leaves proof and the checkout visibly pending.

Do not copy someone else’s buyer count, views, testimonial, or implied track record into the new offer. Demonstrate our own process and examples. Finalize what access includes, how much content is ready, support scope, and refund terms before selling. Whop checkout and course entitlement are the planned delivery mechanism.

For brands

The recurring offer is an ongoing brand-avatar content system. The customer buys a consistent identity, research, a planned production cadence, prompt review, generation within budget, editing, scheduling, and reporting. Ten daily posts should mean ten defined deliverables. Crossposting the same asset three times should not silently become three original videos.

Measure accepted deliverables per day, cost per accepted post, first-pass acceptance, revision rounds, failure rate, publication success, engagement, qualified clicks, revenue attribution when available, support time, client retention, and gross contribution. A recurring service has to pay for the content pipeline even during a low-view month.

Financial model and what the evidence can support

The interactive Economics screen contains editable assumptions for reach, traffic, conversion, pricing, unit costs, returns, affiliate mix, generation yield, footage reuse, review costs, labor, acquisition, churn, and payout delay. Source values and proposed assumptions are separated in the financial notes. The model includes generation retry cost, five-second sample acquisition cost, contribution by offer, shared costs counted once, break-even, a working-capital proxy, and first-month cash impact.

The defaults are operating scenarios, not estimates of Khan’s private business. Under the current base inputs, the combined model produces about $7,062 monthly gross revenue and $859 operating profit before tax. The conservative case loses about $2,062, while the upside case produces about $17,194 profit. Those wide differences are the point of the sensitivity model, not a forecast range with a probability attached.

At the base cost assumptions, each approved post costs about $3.38, and a brand client receiving 300 posts monthly needs roughly $1,141 in monthly revenue just to cover modeled direct costs, payment fees, support, and churn-replacement acquisition. That floor excludes an allocation of all shared company overhead and any target profit margin. A $1,497 planning price leaves about $335 monthly contribution per client under those assumptions.

The important test is whether prompt review changes acceptance yield enough to justify its own cost. The model therefore compares an editable unreviewed yield with reviewed yield. Measure this in real jobs; do not claim a savings percentage before collecting a baseline and a reviewed cohort.

Whop’s published pricing supplies a domestic card baseline of 2.7% plus $0.30. International cards, conversion, and optional services can add costs. The model’s extra-fee input remains editable, and the actual account configuration must be confirmed.

Outreach and customer acquisition

Prioritize products that naturally belong in this audience’s world: keyboards and mice, desk accessories, phone and handheld cases, organizers, collectibles, and original streetwear. A good prospect has a clear product, accessible brand information, an appropriate public business contact route, and a plausible visual concept. A large follower count alone is not qualification.

Exa’s planned role is discovery and evidence retrieval. The app’s server adapter searches for official DTC brand pages and returns source text and public email candidates. It does not invent an email from a name pattern or represent a support inbox as a verified decision maker. All messages remain drafts.

The seed prospect file distinguishes partnership routes, general business contacts, support-only addresses, forms, and unverified candidates. The first five-second sample should use a chosen product reference and an approved claim. Make the sample because the concept fits the brand, not because a generic template can be sent to hundreds of companies.

A draft should show one relevant observation, one original sample concept, and one small next step. Never state that a video is attached or already generated unless it exists. Track research cost, sample cost, replies, qualified conversations, closed clients, and retained contribution. Include the cost of samples for prospects who never buy.

Topic index and coverage checks

The automated topic index matches full machine transcripts and captions against explicit keyword families. Categories overlap. It is a search aid, not a human annotation of every sentence. The evidence JSON supplies exact counts for fantasy-RPG lore, role-play servers, digital economies, nostalgia, relationships, other games/TV, music/trends, internet identity/celebrity satire, cultural-persona sketches, and everyday wordplay. A reel can match several families, and incidental word matches still need source review. Short, repetitive, or suspicious ASR output is flagged instead of treated as dependable dialogue. Remaining unmatched entries are labeled explicitly.

The full index contains all 800 source links, ranks, play counts, durations, dates, topic tags, and transcription status. The 30-video William folder includes the entire million-play subset of this collected sample. Audio and video provenance are retained through per-file source records and hashes. The recovered Higgsfield dossier is a separate private project file with the old avatar, prompts, settings, mistakes, and accepted decisions.
